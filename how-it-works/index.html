Shared Abundance · Building the commons

Productive businesses. Real revenue. Shared results.

Shared Abundance builds and holds productive assets for a shared purpose. Its businesses sell ordinary products and services in the existing economy, using ordinary currencies.

The three steps

Build → automate → allocate actual surplus
  1. Build→
  2. Automate→
  3. Allocate actual surplus
1

Build

Select a useful product or service, define who will pay for it, and test whether the business can cover its costs. Hold productive assets for the shared purpose.

2

Automate

Introduce reliable automation gradually, measuring cost, quality and risk. People continue the judgment and oversight that still require them.

3

Share

Allocate revenue according to published rules and distribute only the amount actually available after costs, obligations, reserves and reinvestment.

Accounting

Where the money goes

Revenue first has to support the business. Costs, taxes and other obligations, reserves, reinvestment and any applicable temporary pioneer commitments must be accounted for before a distribution is calculated.

Expansion

How the model grows

A business creates output that customers pay for. If it produces surplus, some can fund more equipment, better automation or another business. The aim is eventually to finance continued growth from business results.

Membership

Equal member payments

For each distribution period:

Individual payment = distributable surplus ÷ eligible verified members

Eligibility, verification and payment arrangements must still be developed. The aim is ultimately broad human membership, with one equal entitlement per person.

No distributable surplus means no payment for that period. The model should not promise income that its businesses have not produced.

The long-term ambition

Shared Abundance aims for Universal High Income: shared payments that grow well beyond basic subsistence as automation produces sufficient sustainable surplus. That is the destination — not an income promise at launch.

Common questions

Does it need a new currency?

No. Shared Abundance uses ordinary currencies and revenue earned from customers.

Does everything need to be automated immediately?

No. People build and supervise the early businesses. Automation expands only where it performs reliably.

Can someone buy a larger permanent share?

Under the rules, no. Temporary pioneer arrangements may compensate early contributions, but they have defined limits.

Does this require taking over existing businesses?

No. Shared Abundance begins by building new productive capacity alongside existing businesses.

When do payments begin?

When a business generates actual distributable surplus, and the legal, verification and payment arrangements are ready. That work is underway; payments are not claimed before those conditions are met.
