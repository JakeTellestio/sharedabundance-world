Clear rules. Visible changes.

These principles describe the purpose and boundaries of Shared Abundance. They guide how the system is built and how it will operate.

Download principles as text

Governing principles

  1. Broad membership

    The long-term aim is participation by every human being, with practical eligibility, verification and legal arrangements still to be developed.

  2. Equal permanent entitlement

    Each eligible verified person has one equal claim on distributable surplus. A larger payment or contribution cannot purchase a larger permanent entitlement.

  3. Available surplus is shared

    After legitimate costs, taxes, reserves, reinvestment and obligations, distributable surplus flows equally to eligible members under public rules.

  4. An ambitious income objective

    The long-term goal is Universal High Income, dependent on the productive results the system can actually achieve.

  5. Temporary pioneer advantages

    Early contributions may earn agreed compensation or limited returns. Any special financial entitlement has a defined endpoint.

  6. Recognition for founders

    Founders and early contributors may receive lasting recognition, without larger permanent member entitlements.

  7. Money does not buy authority

    Investment, tokens or contributions cannot purchase permanent authority over the governing rules.

  8. Human authority over the rules

    AI cannot rewrite the fundamental rules governing itself. People retain ultimate constitutional authority.

  9. A defined intervention mechanism

    People must have a workable way to stop or override operations when serious problems arise.

  10. Independent checks

    Important decisions should be examined by separate systems or reviewers. No single AI is an unquestionable authority.

  11. Inspectable operations

    Rules, ownership, finances and major decisions should be logged and open to inspection wherever security and privacy permit.

  12. Understandable explanations

    Important rules and financial arrangements must have clear plain-language explanations.

  13. Ordinary currencies

    Businesses can trade in existing currencies. A new currency is not required.

  14. Applicable law and obligations

    Businesses must operate within applicable law and meet legitimate tax and other obligations.

  15. Build additional productive capacity

    The initiative grows shared productive assets alongside existing businesses and ownership.

  16. Accountability for every role

    Founders, investors, developers, operators and AI providers remain subject to the same governing purpose and rules.

  17. Work toward self-funding

    Outside resources support the launch. The aim is to fund continued operation and growth from productive results.

  18. Protect future membership

    Current participants cannot permanently sell away the shared entitlement of future generations.

Still to develop: Legal form and jurisdictions; verification and eligibility; treatment of minors; privacy and security; distribution periods; reserves and reinvestment policy; pioneer terms and their endpoint; rule-amendment procedure; independent review; intervention authority and recovery after a shutdown.

Oversight design

Operational safeguards

Routine operations

AI systems plan and operate business activities within defined limits.

Financial checks

Independent review verifies accounting and supporting evidence.

Risk checks

Important decisions are examined for operational and financial risks.

Rule checks

Decisions are checked against the published governing rules.

Human authority

People retain authority over those rules and a defined emergency intervention mechanism.

Relationship
  1. AI operates within rules→
  2. Independent checks→
  3. Human rule authority & intervention

These safeguards are design requirements. Technical and legal implementation is part of the build ahead.
